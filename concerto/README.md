# The Other Shore
### An Imagined Second Clarinet Concerto · an original composition by Astra

[Enter the listening room](https://isiliconx.github.io/astra6.2/concerto/) · [Full conductor score](edition/conductor-score.pdf) · [Clarinet in A](edition/clarinet-in-a.pdf) · [MusicXML sources](edition/editable-scores.zip) · [MIDI](edition/performance-midi.zip)

The idea is four notes: C♯ rises to A and steps down to G♯ and F♯. The violins offer it before the soloist has a note. Later it is a quiet song, then a dance. This is a new work written for a standard A clarinet, not a piece by Mozart, a catalogue attribution, or a conjectural reconstruction.

## Using the edition
- The **conductor PDF** is at concert pitch. **Clarinet in A** is in written pitch: written C sounds the A below. An A clarinet with a normal low E is sufficient. The solo's complete written range is E3–A6. The top few notes are advanced altissimo.
- The score has eight lines: solo clarinet, flute, bassoon, horn, two violin desks, viola and violoncello. Rehearsal letters restart each movement. The printed metronome marks describe the main pace; the sampled interpretation has smaller tempo inflections. The cadenza is free in character but completely written out. In the 11-page part, the two mid-movement right-hand booklet turns arrive in roughly 11 seconds of solo rest (before I.197 and III.185).
- The MusicXML ZIP contains **nine editable MusicXML 4.0 files**: concert-pitch conductor, transposing conductor and transposed A-clarinet part for each movement. In the transposing conductor edition, written C sounds A on the solo part and F on the horn. The MIDI ZIP includes three movements and a continuous Type 1 performance, each with a tempo track and eight instrument tracks.
- The live reading strip comes from the actual solo engraving, not an approximation from MIDI. The location marker identifies the active measure, not the exact notehead. Counted orchestral rests are divided into their constituent bars in the display.

## Credits and historical reference
The historical point of departure is [Mozart's K. 622 in the Köchel catalogue](https://kv.mozarteum.at/en/work/concerto-in-a-for-clarinet-and-orchestra-7312). We consulted the [1881 Leipzig full score, plate W.A.M. 622](https://s9.imslp.org/files/imglnks/usimg/f/f7/IMSLP29515-PMLP03144-Mozart_Clarinet_Concerto_K622.pdf) for register contrast, orchestral space and concerto scale. No material from the scan is included in these editions. The themes, cadenzas, orchestration and notes here were newly composed.

The original audio is **sampled**, not a live ensemble. Individual notes were realized from 90 sustain and short recordings in [Versilian Studios Chamber Orchestra 2 Community Edition](https://versilian-studios.com/vsco-community/), released by Versilian Studios under [CC0](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/). Its recorded clarinet timbre stands in for the notated A clarinet. Pitch follows the concert manuscript, with notated articulation, dynamics and phrasing; stereo seating and a simulated chamber supply the acoustic. The site uses no audio generation service.

[Composition, engraving and rendering sources](production/). Engraving uses [Verovio](https://www.verovio.org/). The programme illustration is an original digital woodcut-style landscape. Score checks were computational and visual, not a human orchestra reading or a professional player's endorsement.
