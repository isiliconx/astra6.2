Field notes

From the bench
How the clay finds its pitch

Each profile is a stack of circular rings. Their radius, wall thickness and height enter a bending-energy estimate for each mode. A wider body generally rings lower; a thicker wall raises every pitch. “Tune trio” changes the walls rather than shifting the audio.

Check the model against a ring

A uniform cylinder with no axial motion reduces to a ring. Compare the discrete bending calculation with its continuous closed-form solution. This checks the numerical method, not an actual fired pot.

What this model leaves out

Five chosen angular shapes share a single clamped-foot axial profile. Clay properties are uniform; foot contact, glaze stresses, cracking, enclosed air and full shell coupling are not solved. Loudness and decay are designed. No measured pottery tap is used.

The trio is tuned to C major.
